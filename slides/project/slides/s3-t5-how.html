Managed — the right default

You name an embedding endpoint and never run an embedding job. The cost is that model choice is no longer yours to tune.

Self-managed — when you need the model

Domain-specific or fine-tuned embeddings, or a model you are required to use. You now own a pipeline, with the staleness failure that implies.

Metadata either way

Columns you carry are filterable. Decide the filter dimensions at creation — audience, tenant, version, effective date.

Two flows, and the difference between them is one box and a great deal of operational responsibility. In the managed flow, the platform reads the text column, calls the embedding endpoint you named, and maintains the vectors as the table changes. Your side of the picture ends at the Delta table — that is the line to point at. In the self-managed flow, an extra box appears on the left that belongs to you. You compute vectors and write them into a column, and the index syncs that column. You have gained model choice and acquired an embedding job, with exactly the silent-staleness failure mode we warned about earlier: a broken embedding job does not page anyone, it just stops the index learning anything new. Managed is the right default and should be said as such. Reach for self-managed when you genuinely need a specific embedding model, not because owning it feels more rigorous. The third panel is the one to repeat until it is boring: the columns you carry at creation are the only things you can filter on later. Decide your filter dimensions now — audience, tenant, document version, effective date — because adding one means rebuilding. Likely question: "Can I change embedding model later?" It means re-embedding the corpus and, in practice, a new index. Treat the choice as semi-permanent and benchmark before you commit. Timing: 4 minutes.
