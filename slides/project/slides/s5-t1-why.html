Why DDL beats a Python decorator for a tool

What goes wrong with code-only tools

The tool exists in one repository. Its permission check is an if statement in your loop. Another team writing another agent against the same data re-implements the tool, re-implements the check, and gets one of them subtly wrong — and nobody can enumerate who can do what.

The engineering value

A catalog function is discoverable, grantable and auditable by people who have never seen your code. One definition, one permission model, many clients.

Discoverable

It shows up in Catalog Explorer. Other teams find it instead of rebuilding it.

Grantable

Access is a GRANT, reviewable alongside every other permission you hold.

Auditable

Who called it, when, with what. Without you writing any logging.

Real scenario

Three teams build support agents. With code-only tools there are three order-lookup implementations and three different ideas of who may issue a refund. With catalog functions there is one of each, and the difference between the teams is a GRANT someone can read.

This is the third step of the same argument: a rule in the prompt is a request, a rule in code is a control, and a rule in the permission system is a control that outlives your code.

The closing line is the one to lead with and then earn. Session 1 said prompt versus code. Session 5 adds the third step, and the distinction is reach: your code governs your callers, the permission system governs everyone's. The three-teams scenario is not hypothetical in any organisation of size. The duplication is bad; the divergence is worse. Three implementations of "look up an order" will differ in what they return and what they allow, and the only way to compare them is to read three codebases. Spend a moment on auditability, because it is free here and expensive to build. Function invocations are catalog events. You did not write a logging line and you can still answer "who called this, when, with what arguments". Be honest about the trade: DDL is less convenient than a decorator. You need a migration path, a review process and someone who owns the schema. That cost is real and it is the same cost you already pay for tables. Likely question: "What about tools that call an external API?" Register them as Python functions with the credential handling inside, so the caller gets EXECUTE and never sees the secret. That is a strictly better arrangement than an environment variable in every agent. Timing: 4 minutes.
