Why choosing wrong multiplies your cost and your risk

The problem it solves

Teams reach for an agent because the tooling makes it easy, not because the problem needs one. The bill and the incident report arrive later.

An agent wrapped around a fixed task pays for the loop every time, and gives you one fresh chance per request to do something unexpected.

Cost

Tokens multiply by loop count. A 4-step agent costs roughly 4 workflows.

Latency

Each step is a round trip. Users feel every one of them.

Testability

A workflow has paths you can enumerate. An agent has a distribution.

Blast radius

Every tool you expose is something it may call unprompted.

Real scenario — 4,000 invoice PDFs with varying layouts

“The layouts vary” feels like it needs reasoning. It needs a good extractor with a fixed output schema. As an agent: 4,000 loops, 4,000 chances to improvise on a financial document, and no way to diff this month's run against last month's.

The upside is real too — and it is specific: when the next step genuinely depends on what you just found, a workflow has to enumerate every branch, and an agent does not.

This is the commercial argument, and it is the slide a sponsor in the room will remember. Work the four rows as a single sentence: an agent is an architecture where you trade determinism for flexibility, and you pay for that trade in tokens, latency, test coverage and exposure. None of those costs is a reason never to build one. All of them are reasons to know why you did. The invoice scenario is the trap in Lab 1A and it catches most groups. Say the numbers out loud: four thousand documents, a loop that averages three steps, so twelve thousand model calls instead of four thousand — and a financial document is exactly where you least want creative variation. Then be fair to agents. The closing line matters: when the control flow itself depends on what the model finds, a workflow author has to anticipate every branch. That is the case where an agent is not indulgence but the only honest design. Likely question: "Our product manager wants to say we shipped an agent." Then ship a workflow and call the roadmap item an agent. The naming argument is cheaper to lose than the on-call rota. Timing: 4 minutes.
