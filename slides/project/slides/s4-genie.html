About

What this data is and who it is for.

Sources

The tables it may touch. Nothing else is reachable.

Instructions

Business rules in prose. “Revenue excludes cancelled orders.”

Examples

Question → the SQL you would have written.

An empty space is a worse analyst than your junior

Point it at orders with no curation and ask for revenue. It sums every row, including the cancelled ones, and is confidently wrong.

Knowledge mining

Genie reads your query history and proposes instructions and examples from SQL your team already runs. Review them — it is a draft, not an authority.

The work is not prompt engineering. It is writing down the definitions your organisation argues about in a place a machine can read.

Lab 4A is a console walkthrough — every click is screenshotted in the guide, including building the Retail Customer Orders space from an empty workspace. Lead with the failure, not the feature. Create the space, add the orders table, ask "what was our revenue last month", and read the SQL it generates. It sums total_amount across all rows. The cancelled orders are in there. No warning, no hedge — a clean number that is wrong. Then add one instruction: revenue excludes orders with status cancelled. Ask again. The WHERE clause appears. That is the whole lesson about Genie and most of the lesson about agents on enterprise data: the model does not know your business rules, and nothing about its fluency will tell you when it is missing one. Examples are more powerful than instructions for anything involving a join or a date convention. If your fiscal year starts in April, no amount of prose beats one worked example. Expected question: "Who should own the Knowledge Store?" The people who own the metric definitions — not the ML team. This is a data governance artefact that happens to be consumed by a model.
