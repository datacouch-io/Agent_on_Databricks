Why cost control belongs at the boundary, not in each agent

What goes wrong without a gateway

Every team implements its own budget check, its own logging and its own rate limiting — and gets one of them subtly wrong. Nobody can answer “what did we spend on models last month, by team” without a reconciliation exercise, and a looping agent is discovered by the invoice.

The engineering value

A control at the boundary applies to applications you did not write and will not review — the same argument as the GRANT in Session 5, now about spend and safety rather than access.

Observability

One place that knows every model call, including the ones made by other teams.

Containment

A rate limit turns a runaway loop into throttled errors instead of an invoice.

Portability

Switch or add a provider behind one interface, without touching each application.

Real scenario

An agent with no step ceiling is deployed on a Friday. In your own code that is a bug you find on Monday. Behind a gateway with a rate limit it is a throttled endpoint and an alert on Friday afternoon.

Session 5 said a rule in the permission system outlives your code. Unity Gateway is the same principle applied to spend, logging and safety.

A control at the gateway binds every application, including the ones built by the team down the corridor who never read your standards document. Access, spend, logging and safety are all better enforced at a boundary than by convention. The containment panel is the one to dwell on, because it is a failure everybody can picture.
