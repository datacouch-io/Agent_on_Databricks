Why a synced index removes a system you would have owned

What goes wrong without it

The classic RAG stack has a pipeline: read documents, chunk, embed, upsert, handle deletions, backfill after a schema change, and reconcile when it drifts. That pipeline is a production service with on-call implications, and its failure mode is silent staleness — confident answers from documents you retired last month.

The engineering value

Insert into the table, trigger a sync, search. Deletions and updates propagate because Change Data Feed tells the index what moved. There is no embedding job in your repository.

TRIGGERED

You call sync. Cheap, and stale between runs. The right default.

CONTINUOUS

Keeps compute warm and tracks the table closely. Costs more, always current.

The real choice

How stale may an answer be? Answer that in hours, then pick.

Real scenario

A policy clause is withdrawn. In a hand-built pipeline, the deletion has to be propagated to the index — and the most common bug in RAG systems is that it is not, so the agent keeps citing a retired policy with a high similarity score and perfect confidence.

Lead with the pipeline you are not building, because people who have built one will recognise every item in that list. The silent-staleness failure is the one to dwell on. A broken embedding pipeline does not page you. Searches keep succeeding, scores stay high, and the agent cites a document that no longer reflects policy. There is no error anywhere in the system, and you find out when a customer quotes your agent back at you. On triggered versus continuous: resist making this a cost conversation first. The question is how stale an answer may be, expressed in hours. A product catalogue might tolerate a day. A pricing policy might not tolerate an hour. Once you have that number, the choice makes itself, and triggered on a schedule covers most cases. The withdrawn-clause scenario is the concrete version of the staleness point and it is worth asking the room whether their current system handles deletions. Many do not know. Likely question: "How quickly does a triggered sync complete?" Small corpora, under a minute; the lab waits in a loop and prints status. Plan for it being minutes, not milliseconds, and never make it synchronous with a user request. Timing: 4 minutes.
