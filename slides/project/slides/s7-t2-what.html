What is Agent Bricks?

Agent Bricks builds agents for you from a description and your data. A Knowledge Assistant is the retrieval-focused kind: point it at documents or an index, and it produces a working, deployed assistant.

Console only

Created and configured in the workspace UI. There is no REST API for it.

Two endpoints

It provisions both the assistant and its supporting endpoint for you.

Optimised for you

Prompting, retrieval settings and evaluation are chosen by the product.

Premium feature

Not available on a trial workspace — /ml/bricks returns Page not found.

Where it fits honestly

Fastest path from documents to a usable assistant, and the least control over how it retrieves. A good baseline, a good internal tool, and a thing to evaluate rather than assume.

The analogy

A managed service against a hand-rolled one. You get working infrastructure in minutes and you accept someone else's defaults.

Agent Bricks is genuinely impressive and the demonstration in two slides is unflattering, so the framing matters: the problem it reveals is not a product defect, it is an inheritance question. Three practical facts. It is console-only, so it does not fit a scripted deployment pipeline the way a logged model does. It provisions two endpoints, which is worth knowing when you look at your cost report. And it needs Premium — the Agents entry appears in the left navigation on a trial workspace and the page itself returns Page not found, which is a nice illustration that a navigation entry is not a feature. The white panel is the honest placement. If someone needs an assistant over a document set this week, this is the fastest route and the answers are good. What you give up is control over retrieval — including which filters are applied, which is the whole subject of the next two slides. Analogy: a managed service versus hand-rolled. Minutes instead of days, and someone else's defaults. Likely question: "Can I export what it built?" No. Treat it as a product you configure, not a scaffold you take over.
