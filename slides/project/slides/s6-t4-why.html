Why aliases make rollback a decision, not a project

What goes wrong without them

Rollback becomes a redeployment: find the previous version, rebuild or re-register it, update every client, verify. Twenty minutes of work in the best case, under incident pressure, by someone who may not have deployed it originally. So teams hesitate — and hesitating during an incident is the actual cost.

The engineering value

If reverting is one metadata change, you will do it early and argue about the cause afterwards. That single property changes how willing a team is to ship at all.

Fast

Seconds, not a deployment cycle. No artefact is rebuilt.

Safe

The previous version is the exact artefact that was working, not a rebuild of it.

Blameless

“Move the alias, investigate after” removes the judgement call from the stressful moment.

Real scenario

A canary shows the challenger refusing questions the champion answered. With aliases you revert in seconds and read the traces at leisure. In Lab 7B the right answer turned out to be “do not promote” — and reverting cost nothing, so making that call was easy.

Cheap reversal is what makes careful deployment possible. Teams that cannot roll back quickly do not ship cautiously — they ship rarely, and then all at once.

This slide is about organisational behaviour as much as tooling, and the closing line is the argument. Make the point about hesitation explicitly. The cost of slow rollback is not the twenty minutes. It is that someone looks at an ambiguous signal and decides to wait and gather more data, because reverting is expensive and might be unnecessary. That decision is usually wrong, and it is a rational response to an expensive rollback. Invert it: if reverting is one metadata change, the correct move under uncertainty is always to revert and investigate afterwards. No judgement required in the stressful moment, which is precisely when judgement is worst. The last panel — blameless — is worth naming. Cheap rollback removes a conversation about whether this is bad enough to warrant reverting. There is no threshold to argue about. The closing line is the one to leave up. Teams without fast rollback do not become more careful; they become slower and batch their risk into large, infrequent releases, which is strictly worse.
