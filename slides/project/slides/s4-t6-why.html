Why an ontology is the answer to “it works in the demo”

What goes wrong without one

Every AI surface is curated separately. Someone writes the revenue rule into a Genie Agent, someone else writes it into an agent's prompt, and the coding assistant knows neither. Three curation efforts, three drift rates, and no way to tell which surface is currently wrong.

The engineering value

One context layer, many experiences. Curate once and Genie One, Genie Code and the agents that call them share it — with authority scoring and conflict resolution doing the work you would otherwise do by hand.

Accuracy

Conflicts resolved by authority rather than by whichever text was pasted most recently.

Security

Permissions enforced during retrieval, on the underlying sources. Two users, two answers.

Effort

Inferred context reuses curation you already did, in assets built for other reasons.

The honest caveat

Inferred context inherits your organisation's habits — including the bad ones. A dashboard with a wrong metric is a source. The modelled layer is how you outrank it: certify what is right, deprecate what is not.

Why this matters beyond being a nice architecture: it is the difference between an AI demo and an AI surface area you can run. The failure in the first panel is one organisations reach within about two quarters. Each AI surface gets curated by whoever owns it, the definitions drift apart, and nobody can say which one is currently correct. An ontology makes curation a shared asset instead of a per-product chore. The security panel deserves a sentence of its own because it is easy to miss. Permissions are enforced during retrieval against the underlying sources. Two employees asking an identical question can correctly receive different answers. That is the property that makes a company-wide assistant possible at all. Then the caveat, which keeps this honest. Inferred context is extracted from what you have, and what you have includes a dashboard with a wrong metric and a SQL query someone wrote badly in 2023. Those are sources too. The lever is the modelled layer: certify what is right, deprecate what is not, and let authority scoring do the rest. That is a nice reframing to leave them with — certification stops being paperwork and becomes a direct input to answer quality. Likely question: "Does this replace the Knowledge Store on a Genie Agent?" No. The Genie Agent's own instructions and examples remain, and they are one of the sources the ontology draws on.
