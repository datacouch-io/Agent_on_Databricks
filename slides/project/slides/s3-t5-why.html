Why chunking sets the ceiling on everything downstream

What goes wrong

Chunks too large: every result is mostly irrelevant text, context cost rises, and the model has to find the answer inside the noise. Chunks too small: the clause is retrieved without the condition that qualifies it, and the agent answers confidently from half a rule.

Why tuning cannot rescue it

Search ranks what you gave it. If the right answer is not a retrievable unit, no embedding model, no top-k and no reranker produces it.

Too large

30-page documents as rows. High scores, low usefulness, expensive context.

Too small

A sentence without its condition. Correct text, wrong meaning.

About right

One clause with its heading and its qualifiers. Stands alone when read cold.

Real scenario

A returns policy is split mid-clause. One chunk says refunds are available within 30 days; the next holds “… except for personalised items”. Retrieval returns the first. The agent is accurate, grounded, cites a real document — and wrong.

The test for a chunk: read it cold, with no surrounding text. Does it still mean what it means?

56

The scenario is the one to lead with if the room is sceptical that chunking deserves a slide. A policy clause split so that the exception lands in the next chunk. Retrieval works. Grounding scores well — the answer is supported by the retrieved text. The citation points at a real document. And the customer is told they can return a personalised item. Every quality signal you have is green. That is why this cannot be fixed downstream. Your groundedness scorer in Session 6 will pass it, because the answer is faithful to what was retrieved. The defect is in what was retrievable. The three-panel comparison gives them the shape of the trade. Too large wastes context and buries the answer. Too small severs meaning from its qualifiers. The useful target is a unit that survives being read cold. That cold-read test is the takeaway and it is a practice people can apply the same afternoon: sample twenty chunks at random, read them with no context, and ask whether each still means what it should. Likely question: "What about overlapping chunks?" Overlap helps with severed qualifiers and costs duplication in results. Try it, and measure with a benchmark rather than by eye. Timing: 4 minutes.
