Why latency decides whether the agent gets used

What goes wrong

An agent that takes fourteen seconds to answer is abandoned, routed around, or quietly replaced by the old process. Adoption fails for a reason nobody records as a defect, and the quality work you did is never experienced by anyone.

The engineering value

Latency is the cheapest adoption lever you have. Streaming a first token in under two seconds changes the experience without changing a single answer.

Interactive

A human is waiting. First token under ~2s, completion under ~10s.

Assisted

A human reviews afterwards. Seconds to a minute is fine.

Batch

Nobody waits. Optimise throughput and cost, not latency.

Real scenario

An agent answered well in eleven seconds and support staff stopped using it. The slow part was a tool making three sequential lookups that were independent of one another. Running them in parallel took it to four. Nothing about the answers changed.

Classify the workload before optimising. Most latency effort is spent on agents where nobody was waiting.
