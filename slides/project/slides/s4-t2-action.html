Walk the four tabs in order, because the order is also the priority. About is one or two sentences and people skip it. Do not — it is how the model knows the grain of the table. "One row per order" prevents a surprising amount of nonsense. Sources is the security boundary. Say explicitly that this is not a hint; tables outside the space are not reachable. Instructions is where the business lives, and the second one on the slide is the under-rated kind: excluding test accounts. Almost every warehouse has them, almost nobody documents them, and they quietly distort every customer-level answer. Examples is where you spend your effort for anything with a join. Then show the before-and-after. Two classes of defect eliminated with three entries and no deployment. That is the slide's argument, and the "for every caller" clause is the one that matters when Lab 4B arrives. Likely question: "Should this live in the console or in version control?" Author in the console, keep the source of truth in your repository, apply through the API. Treat instructions and examples as code — they change behaviour and they deserve review.
