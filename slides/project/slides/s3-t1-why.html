Why one governance boundary beats five good products

What goes wrong with five vendors

A hosted model, a vector database, a tracing service, a metrics store and a registry. Each one is defensible. Together they are five permission models, and the question “who was allowed to see the document that produced this answer?” becomes a research project with no reliable answer.

The engineering value

The table, the index, the tool, the trace and the model are all Unity Catalog objects — Databricks' governance layer for data and AI assets. One grant model covers retrieval, actions and audit.

Lineage

The index knows its source table. The trace knows which documents grounded the answer.

Access

Revoke someone's access to a table and the agent's retrieval narrows with it.

Audit

Traces land in a catalog you own, as a table. Not a vendor's dashboard with an export button.

Real scenario

An auditor asks which internal documents reached customer-facing answers last quarter. With traces in Unity Catalog that is one SQL query joining spans to document ids. Across five products it is five export requests and a spreadsheet nobody trusts.

This is the platform argument, and it should be made honestly rather than as a sales pitch. Every component in that stack has a credible standalone competitor, and some of them are better at their one job. What you cannot buy separately is the boundary. Say the auditor scenario out loud — it is the question that actually arrives, usually with a deadline, and it is the one that five best-of-breed tools answer badly. Not because any of them is bad, but because the join between them exists only in someone's head. The lineage point is the one engineers care about: the index knows its source table, so a column-level change is traceable to the retrieval behaviour it altered. The access point is the one security teams care about: permissions are not re-implemented in your agent code. Be fair about the trade-off, because the room will think it: you are accepting a platform dependency. The mitigation is Session 2's seam — your agent logic stays portable even when your governance does not. Likely question: "We already have a vector database we like." Keep it behind the seam and compare. The question is not which search is better; it is whether you can answer the auditor. Timing: 4 minutes.
