Why context is the main driver of cost, latency and quality

What goes wrong without managing it

Context grows every turn, so a four-step agent pays for the whole conversation four times. Then a tool returns a large result, it lands in messages, and every subsequent turn carries it. Nothing errors. The agent just becomes slow, expensive, and less accurate — in that order, and usually unnoticed.

Why quality degrades too

Relevant facts compete with irrelevant ones for the model's attention. Twenty retrieved documents where three are relevant is worse than three, not merely more expensive.

Cost

Input tokens are re-paid each turn. Context × steps, not context + steps.

Latency

Time to first token grows with input length. Users feel the padding.

Accuracy

Facts buried mid-context are attended to least. This is a measured effect, not folklore.

Real scenario

An agent's search_policy returns the top ten passages instead of three. Cost per conversation nearly doubles, p95 latency rises, and answers get vaguer — because the model now has seven more plausible-looking passages to reconcile.

Lead with the multiplication, because it is the part people get wrong in their head. Context is not paid once per conversation; it is paid once per model call, and an agent makes several. A four-step agent with a growing conversation pays for roughly the whole history four times. Then the counter-intuitive half, which is the more valuable idea. More context is not neutral-but-expensive — past a point it is actively worse. Three relevant documents beat twenty documents of which three are relevant, because the model has to decide what matters and will sometimes decide wrong. There is published work on attention degrading for material in the middle of long inputs; you do not need the citation, just the behaviour. The scenario is a change anyone might make: raise top-k from three to ten to "improve recall". Cost up, latency up, and answers measurably vaguer. It is a good example of an intervention that looks like an improvement and tests as a regression — which is why Session 6 exists. Likely question: "How do I know if context is my problem?"
