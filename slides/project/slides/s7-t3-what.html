What is a canary deployment?

A canary sends a small share of real traffic to a new version while the rest continues to the current one, so you compare them on live requests rather than on a test set.

A traffic split

Real requests

The questions users actually ask, including the ones you never thought to write.

A control group

The 90% is not just safety — it is the comparison that makes the 10% readable.

Define it precisely, because people use "canary" loosely for "deploy and watch". A canary is a split: a defined share of live traffic to the new version, the remainder to the current one, at the same time. Both halves matter. The 90% is usually described as the safety property — most users are unaffected if the new version is bad — and the third card makes the more interesting point: it is also the control group. Without concurrent traffic on the old version you are comparing this week's challenger against last week's champion, on different questions, possibly different data, and you have no way to separate the change from the conditions. The white panel is the argument for canaries over simply writing more evaluation cases. Your dataset is a list of questions you were able to imagine. Production contains misspellings, two questions in one sentence, and enquiries about products you retired in 2024. No amount of imagination substitutes. Analogy: a taste test with both glasses on the table. Likely question: "What split should I start with?" Small enough that a bad version is survivable, large enough to see a difference in a reasonable window. Ten percent is a sensible default; the real constraint is your traffic volume.
