What to do in your first week back

Day 1

Pick one candidate and apply the decision test. If it is a workflow, say so and build that instead.

Day 2

Instrument something you already have. One @mlflow.trace decorator and a Unity Catalog destination.

Day 3

Write the aggregate span query and look at it. Latency by tool, failures by tool.

Day 4

Build a twenty-row evaluation set, including four cases where the right answer is “no”.

Day 5

Run it, open every zero, and read the rationale. Decide each time who was wrong.

Then, when you have a real agent

Register its tools as Unity Catalog functions, give it a service principal with the smallest viable grants, and rehearse a rollback on a quiet afternoon.

What you already have

Thirteen notebooks with real outputs, fifteen lab guides, and a teardown script. All of it runs against your own workspace.

Make this concrete, because the gap between a course and a changed practice is usually that nobody did anything in the first week. The five days are deliberately small. Day one costs an hour and may save a quarter. Day two is one decorator. Day three is one query. Days four and five are the ones that matter most and the ones people skip, because building an evaluation set feels like overhead until the first time it catches something. Emphasise the marked rows. Four cases where the right answer is "no" — out of scope, policy forbids it, missing information, ambiguous request. Those are where agents fail in ways that embarrass you, and where your judge is most likely to be wrong. Building them on day four means discovering both problems in a week rather than a quarter. Remind them what they are taking home: the notebooks have saved outputs, so they are readable as references even without re-running them, and the teardown script means they can experiment without leaving infrastructure behind. Likely question: "What if my organisation is not on Databricks?" Days one to five are platform-independent. The decision test, tracing, the span query, the evaluation set and reading rationales all transfer directly; only the product names change. Timing: 4 minutes.
