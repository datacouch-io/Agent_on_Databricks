What is an MLflow trace?

A trace is a structured record of one agent interaction: a tree of spans, where each span is one operation with its inputs, outputs, timing and status. MLflow is Databricks' open-source experiment-tracking and model-management framework.

One decorator

@mlflow.trace on a function. Its arguments and return value are captured.

Span types

AGENT for the whole turn, TOOL for one call, RETRIEVER for what came back.

A destination

Set it to a Unity Catalog schema and traces land in tables you own.

Queryable

Two tables: one for traces, one for spans. Ordinary SQL.

Why span types are not cosmetic

A RETRIEVER span renders retrieved documents in the UI, and MLflow's retrieval scorers in Session 6 look for it by type. Mislabel it and the evaluation silently has nothing to grade.

The analogy

A flight data recorder. Not a log line saying “landed”, but every input and control movement, nested, timed, and readable afterwards.

Define span before trace, because the word does the work. A span is one operation with its inputs, outputs, duration and status. A trace is the tree of spans for one interaction. If you have used OpenTelemetry this is familiar — and it is literally OpenTelemetry underneath, which matters on the next slide. The second card and the white panel are the same point and it is the one people get wrong. span_type is not a label for humans. It is a contract: RETRIEVER spans are what render retrieved chunks in the UI, and what MLflow's built-in retrieval metrics look for. Mislabelling a retriever as a generic span does not error — your evaluation just quietly measures nothing. Push on the destination card too. Traces can go to an experiment, or to a Unity Catalog schema. Choosing the catalog is what turns observability from a vendor dashboard into a table in your own governance boundary — which was the argument three slides ago.
