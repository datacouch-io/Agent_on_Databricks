How to cut tokens without cutting quality

1 · Tighten retrieval

Lower top-k, add a score floor, improve chunking. Usually the largest single win.

2 · Shrink tool output

Return decided answers — named columns, one row — not result sets.

3 · Trim tool schemas

They are re-sent every turn. Fewer, better-described tools beat many thin ones.

4 · Manage the conversation

Summarise past a threshold; evict finished sub-task output.

5 · Order for caching

Stable prefix first, variable material last, where caching is available.

6 · Right-size the model

Last, not first — and only with the evaluation set open.

Why model choice is last

It is the change with the widest blast radius and the easiest to justify badly. Exhaust the structural wins first; they carry no quality risk.

Measure the right thing

Tokens per resolved request, with the evaluation score beside it. A 40% cost cut that loses two benchmark cases is a decision, not a win — make it explicitly.

Six techniques in priority order, and the order is the content. Most teams start at six and never get to one. Retrieval is first because it is usually the biggest line and the safest to change. Lowering top-k from ten to three, adding a score floor, and fixing chunking will often halve input tokens and improve answers at the same time. Tool output is second and it is the one with the pathological cases. A tool that can return a large result set is both your biggest cost risk and your biggest quality risk. Tool schemas are third and almost nobody looks at them. They are re-sent on every single request, so twenty thin tools cost real money against eight well-described ones — and selection quality improves too. Conversation management is fourth: summarise past a threshold, evict what is finished. Caching order is fifth and free: invariant material first. Model choice is last, deliberately. It is the widest-blast-radius change and the easiest to justify badly under budget pressure. Exhaust the structural wins first — they carry no quality risk at all. The bottom bar is the discipline. Report the cost change and the evaluation score together, always. Likely question: "How much can we usually save?" Halving input tokens through retrieval and tool-output work is common, before touching the model. Timing: 4 minutes.
