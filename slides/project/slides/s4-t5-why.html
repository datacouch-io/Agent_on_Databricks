Why definitions belong in the catalog, not in each consumer

What goes wrong without them

“Revenue” is implemented in a dashboard, re-implemented in a notebook, re-implemented in a Genie instruction, and approximated in a spreadsheet. Four definitions, four owners, no way to tell which is right — and every AI consumer inherits whichever one it happened to see.

The engineering value

One definition, with an owner, a grant and lineage. Change it once and every consumer — dashboards, SQL, Genie One, your agents — changes with it.

For humans

A certified metric view ends the quarterly argument about whose number is right.

For Genie

A governed definition beats a prose instruction, because it executes rather than persuades.

For agents

Your agent inherits the definition without being told it exists. Session 4's whole thesis.

This is the course's control lesson again

A rule in a prompt is a request. A rule in your code binds your callers. A metric view binds everyone who queries the metric — including consumers written next year by people who never read your instruction.
