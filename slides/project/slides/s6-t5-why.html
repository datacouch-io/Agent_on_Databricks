Why cost work is quality work in disguise

What goes wrong when cost is ignored

A pilot at a hundred conversations a day is affordable at any level of waste. The same agent at fifty thousand a day is a budget conversation, and the usual response — swapping to a cheaper model under time pressure — is made with no evaluation set to tell you what it broke.

The engineering value

Most token waste is also quality waste. Over-retrieval, over-long tool output and unnecessary steps make answers worse as well as dearer. Fixing cost usually improves accuracy.

Over-retrieval

Ten passages where three are relevant: more cost, vaguer answers.

Fat tool output

Raw result sets in context: more cost, diluted attention.

Extra steps

Granular tools force more loops: more cost, more chances to go wrong.

Real scenario

Raising top-k from 3 to 10 to “improve recall” nearly doubled cost per conversation and made answers measurably hedgier. The cost review found it; the quality review had missed it for a month.

Do cost work with your evaluation set open. Otherwise you are trading quality for money without knowing the exchange rate.

The insight on this slide is the one that makes cost work worth an engineer's time rather than a finance task. Say it directly: the three main sources of token waste are also sources of quality loss. Over-retrieval dilutes attention. Fat tool output buries the relevant fact. Extra steps multiply the opportunities for a wrong decision. So a cost-reduction exercise, done properly, usually comes back with better answers as well as a smaller bill. The scenario is the same top-k change from Session 1's context topic, seen from the other side — and the detail that matters is who caught it. The quality review had been running for a month and missed it, because the aggregate score barely moved. The cost review caught it in a day, because cost is unambiguous. That is a nice argument for watching both: cost is a blunt but honest instrument, and in agents it correlates with the things that are hard to measure. The closing instruction is the practice. Never do cost work without the evaluation set open. Otherwise you are trading quality for money at an exchange rate you cannot see — and the trade is often fine, but it should be a decision. Likely question: "What is a reasonable cost per conversation?" There is no general answer. Benchmark against what the interaction is worth — a deflected support ticket has a known value, and that is the number to compare against. Timing: 3 minutes.
