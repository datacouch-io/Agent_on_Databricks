How one improvement cycle actually runs

1 · Observe

a complaint, or a metric that moved

2 · Diagnose

open the traces behind the failing cases

3 · Attribute

retrieval, selection, context or generation?

4 · Change one thing

and record what you changed

5 · Re-run

same dataset, same scorers, compare per case

Step 3 is the step people skip

Four causes produce the same bad answer and need four different fixes. The trace distinguishes them; the final text does not.

Step 4 means one thing

Change the prompt and the dataset together and you have learned nothing. This is the discipline, not a formality.

Step 5 means per case

Read the rows, not the mean. Segment answerable from unanswerable. Open every zero.

What a finished cycle looks like

“Retrieval scored below 0.4 on returns questions. The policy document had no damaged-goods clause. We added it, re-ran, and 3 cases moved from fail to pass with no regressions.” That sentence is the deliverable.

Why it is worth writing down

It is the evidence a reviewer needs, and it is what stops the same diagnosis being redone next quarter.

This is the outline's "trace-based diagnosis and a measured improvement cycle", and it is the slide that ties Sessions 3 and 6 together. Walk the five steps, then stop on three, four and five — the ones teams compress. Step three is attribution and it is where the trace earns its existence. Retrieval returned nothing useful; the model picked the wrong tool; the context was bloated and the fact got lost; the generation was poor despite good inputs. Four causes, one symptom, four different fixes. The final answer cannot tell you which. The trace can, in about a minute. Step four means one thing at a time, and it is worth being slightly severe about. Everyone changes the prompt and adds two dataset rows in the same sitting, because both felt obviously good. The comparison is then worthless. Step five is Session 6's whole argument in one line: read the rows, not the mean. The bottom-left bar is what you should ask people to produce. That sentence — what was wrong, what the trace showed, what changed, what moved — is the deliverable. It is the evidence for a reviewer and it is what stops the same investigation happening again in six months. Likely question: "How long should a cycle take?" Hours, not weeks, once the dataset and scorers exist. Building them is the one-off cost. Timing: 4 minutes.
