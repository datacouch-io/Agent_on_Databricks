Session 3 opens by introducing Databricks itself, which the outline asks for and which is easy to skip in a room of engineers who have used notebooks. The diagram is the platform's thesis and it is worth reading aloud. The top row is what a foundation model arrives with — language, code, public knowledge, general reasoning. The bottom row is what it can only get from you: your schema, your definitions, your permissions, your history. Every component in the next five sessions exists to supply the second row. Land the example in the second row. A model knows what the word revenue means in English. It does not know that your revenue excludes cancelled orders. No amount of model capability closes that gap; only your data and your semantics do. The four layers give people a map. Lakehouse is one copy of the data in Delta. Unity Catalog governs not just tables but functions, indexes, models and traces — and that breadth is what makes Session 5's argument work. Compute is serverless for everything we do here. AI is the layer this course lives in. Keep this short. It is orientation, not content, and the room will feel it if you linger. Likely question: "Do we need all of it?" No. The labs use Unity Catalog, serverless compute, a SQL warehouse, AI Search, Foundation Models and MLflow. The pre-flight slide lists exactly that. Timing: 3 minutes.
