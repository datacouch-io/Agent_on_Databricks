Choosing a shape — and what each one costs

Manager – Workers

Reach for it when requests fall into clear domains, or workers need different identities. It costs one extra model call per request. It fails by routing correctly and handing off incompletely — Lab 7A’s result.

Planner – Executor

Reach for it when the task is multi-step and the plan should be inspectable, approved or diffed before anything runs. It costs a planning round trip. It fails wholesale: a plausible plan executed faithfully, every step correct, the goal wrong.

Hierarchical

Reach for it when one supervisor can no longer hold the tool set, or separate teams own separate branches. It costs latency and context at every level. It fails by dilution — each hop passes a little less than it received.

Swarm – peer handoff

Reach for it when control should follow the conversation rather than a fixed map. It costs you the single routing record a trace is easiest to read from. It fails by circling — two agents handing the same request back and forth, with no owner for the stopping condition.

The order to try them in

One agent. Then manager–workers. Everything beyond that needs a reason you can say out loud — tool count, identity, ownership or model — and a bounded loop at every level that can route.

Four patterns, four distinct ways to be confidently wrong. Planner-executor deserves the warning it gets. Every individual step can be correct and the outcome still wrong, because the mistake was made once, early, and then followed obediently. That failure is invisible in step-level traces and obvious in the plan - which is the argument for writing the plan down. Swarm is the one people are most attracted to and least ready for. Ask who owns MAX_STEPS in a swarm. There is no good answer, which is the answer. Tie it back: the reasons to split are the four from the previous slide - tools, identity, owner, model.
