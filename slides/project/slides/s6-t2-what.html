What is an LLM judge?

An LLM judge — called a scorer in MLflow — is a model prompted to grade another model's output against a criterion, returning a value and a rationale explaining it.

A value

Usually pass or fail, or a number. This is what lands in your aggregate.

A rationale

Prose explaining the verdict. The most under-read field in the entire workflow.

Built-in scorers

Correctness, RelevanceToQuery, Safety, Guidelines and more.

Custom guidelines

Your rules in prose, evaluated per case by Guidelines.

It is a model, with everything that implies

It has a prompt you did not write, it can misread the question, and it is confident either way. A judge is a measurement instrument, and instruments need calibrating.

The analogy

A second marker who has read the rubric but not sat the exam. Useful, scalable, and occasionally wrong in ways the candidate can see and the marker cannot.

Define scorer and judge as the same thing, because MLflow says scorer and the field says judge and people get lost. The white panel is the whole slide. A judge is a language model with a prompt, and everything you know about language models applies to it: it can misread the question, it can apply a rule that was not in scope, and it reports its verdict with the same confidence either way. Teams treat scorer output as measurement and it is closer to a second opinion. Then raise the rationale, which is the most important and least read field in this workflow. The value goes into your aggregate and your dashboard. The rationale says why, and it is the only thing that tells you whether the judge understood the case. Reading values without rationales is how all four of this course's broken judgements survived their first look. Analogy: a second marker who has read the rubric but not sat the exam. Likely question: "Which model judges?" A strong one — judging is harder than answering, and a cheap judge produces noise you will mistake for signal. Spend on the judge. Timing: 3 minutes.
