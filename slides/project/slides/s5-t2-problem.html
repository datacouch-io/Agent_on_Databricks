Do not skip to the protocol. Spend a minute on the arithmetic, because it is what makes MCP feel inevitable rather than fashionable. Four clients, five tool sets, twenty integrations - and the number grows multiplicatively, so the second client is cheap and the fifth is not. Ask the room how many internal "connector" repositories they own. The answer is usually more than they expected. The middle card is the one engineers feel. The same tool described in four codebases is four prompts, because a tool description is prompt text. They drift, and the drift shows up as the model picking the wrong tool in one client and not another. Then the turn: MCP does not make your tools safer. It makes them reachable - and that is precisely why the next slide is about who is asking. Expected question: "Is this just OpenAPI again?" Fair challenge. The difference is that the contract is written for a model rather than a programmer: descriptions, not just types, and discovery that depends on the caller's identity. Timing: 4 minutes.
