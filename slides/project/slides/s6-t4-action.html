See it in action: three aliases, one lever

# before promoting anything
c.set_registered_model_alias(
  name=NAME, alias="previous",
  version=current_champion)

# promote the candidate
c.set_registered_model_alias(
  name=NAME, alias="champion",
  version=challenger)

# ... and the way back
c.set_registered_model_alias(
  name=NAME, alias="champion",
  version=previous)

Clients never changed

Everything referencing @champion followed the lever. No redeploy, no client update, no coordination.

Rehearse it

Do the rollback once, deliberately, on a day when nothing is wrong. A rollback path you have never executed is a rollback path you do not have.

Three calls. The useful thing is the order, so read the comments rather than the code. Set @previous first, pointing at the current champion. Then move @champion to the challenger. Those two statements mean that at every moment there is a named, known-good version to go back to. Teams routinely do the second without the first and then have to reconstruct which version was good. The output panel makes the key property visible: after rollback, champion points at v3 again and no artefact was rebuilt. It is the same bytes that were serving traffic an hour ago, not a rebuild that might differ. The "rehearse it" note is the practice worth insisting on. Run the rollback once on a quiet day. Almost every team's first real rollback is also their first rollback, performed under pressure, and discovers something — a cached client, an alias set on the wrong model name, a permission nobody had. Find that out on a Tuesday afternoon instead. Likely question: "Should promotion be automated on a metric?" Not initially. Automate the alias mechanics and keep a human on the decision until you trust your evaluation — and Session 6 has just spent forty minutes explaining why you should not trust it yet.
