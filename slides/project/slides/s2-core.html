Lab 2A is where the course stops being a tutorial and starts being an architecture. Point at the two retrieval modes first. A refund question needs both: what did this customer actually buy, and what does policy permit? One is a lookup by key, the other is a similarity search over prose. Agents that only do RAG answer the second question and guess at the first. Then the discipline. core.py may import json, os and the OpenAI client — nothing else. Everything vendor-specific lives in the retriever passed into it. The lab enforces this with an AST check over core.py's imports. A note on that check: an earlier version of it was a grep for the string "databricks", which reported a leak. The hit was inside core.py's own docstring, describing what it must not import. The lab keeps both checks and labels the grep result a known false positive — a small, early example of the theme that runs through this course: read what the instrument actually measured. Expected question: "Why not use a framework for this?" You can, and Session 7 does. The point of doing it by hand once is that you then know what the framework is deciding on your behalf.
