Why an endpoint, and not a notebook

What goes wrong with a notebook in production

It is bound to one user's identity, one cluster's state and whatever that user last edited. There is no version, no rollback, no request log and no way for a second system to call it. Every reliability property you want is absent, and the first incident is also the first time anyone notices.

The engineering value

A deployed version is an artefact: immutable, named, logged, and replaceable without touching code. That is what makes canaries, rollback and A/B comparison possible at all.

Callable

By your app, another agent, a scheduled job, or a colleague with curl.

Observable

Requests and responses land in tables. Traces continue to work.

Reversible

A version you can point away from in seconds. Session 6's last topic.

Real scenario

An agent is answering customers from a notebook attached to an engineer's cluster. The engineer goes on leave, the cluster terminates, and the agent stops. Nobody can redeploy it, because the version that was running exists only as unsaved cells.

The deployment boundary is where an experiment becomes a service. Everything in Session 7 assumes you crossed it.

Callable matters because it unlocks every integration — an app, a job, another agent. Observable matters because requests and responses land in tables, so you can answer questions about real traffic rather than your test set. Reversible is the one people underrate until the day they need it, and it is the subject of the next topic. The word to emphasise is immutable. A logged model version is a frozen artefact: the code, the dependencies, the configuration. That is what lets you compare two versions honestly and roll back to one with confidence. A notebook has none of those properties, by design — its whole value is that you can change it. Close on the last line, because it sets up Session 7. Canaries, traffic splitting and rollback all operate on deployed versions. Without the endpoint, none of the operational content applies.
