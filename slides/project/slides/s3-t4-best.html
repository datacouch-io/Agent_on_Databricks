Tracing — do and don't

Do

Set span_type deliberately. RETRIEVER is what the Session 6 scorers look for.

Send traces to a Unity Catalog schema, so observability sits inside your governance boundary.

Trace the tool implementations, not just the agent entry point.

Write the aggregate query on day one and keep it. It is your agent's vital signs.

Sample in production if volume demands it — but sample, do not switch off.

Join spans to business tables to measure outcomes, not just latency.

Don't

Don't put secrets or raw personal data in span inputs. A trace is a table other people can read.

Don't query status_code. It is status.code — these are OpenTelemetry spans.

Don't rely on the UI alone. It answers one interaction, never a trend.

Don't add tracing after an incident. It is retroactive on nothing.

Don't trace enormous tool outputs verbatim if you also care about span size and cost.

Don't assume an OK status means a good answer. It means nothing threw.

The last “don't” is the bridge to Session 6. Every span can be OK, every tool can succeed, every latency can be healthy — and the answer can still be wrong. Status tells you the machinery worked. Quality needs a different instrument.

69

Close Session 3 here. Two lines deserve airtime. The privacy "don't" is first for a reason. Span inputs are captured automatically, which is the feature, and it means whatever you passed into a traced function is now in a table with its own grants and its own retention. If a tool takes a customer identifier or a free-text message, you have made a decision about personal data whether or not you meant to. Decide it deliberately, redact at the boundary, and grant the trace schema accordingly. The status "don't" is the bridge and the better closing note. OK means no exception was raised. It says nothing about whether the retrieval was relevant, the tool choice sensible or the answer true. Teams build a healthy-looking dashboard out of span statuses and believe they are measuring quality. They are measuring plumbing. That gap is what Session 6 exists for — and it is also where the course's running theme returns, because the quality instruments turn out to need checking too. Likely question: "How long should we keep traces?" Long enough to investigate a complaint and to compare a deployment against the one before it. Treat it as a data retention decision with a stated reason, not a default. Timing: 3 minutes, then break.
