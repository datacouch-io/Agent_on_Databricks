Why Genie, rather than a text-to-SQL prompt of your own

What goes wrong with a home-made version

A model with your schema in the prompt writes plausible SQL and no business rules. It will sum a column called total_amount and call it revenue. You then build the missing pieces yourself: scope limits, stored definitions, example queries, a regression suite, and somewhere to keep them.

The engineering value

Those pieces are the product. Scope, Knowledge Store, examples and benchmarks exist so that curation has a home other than a prompt string in someone's repository.

Scope is enforced

By table selection and Unity Catalog grants, not by an instruction asking nicely.

Rules are inherited

Written once in the space, applied to every caller — including agents.

Quality is measurable

Benchmarks give you a number that moves when you change an instruction.

Real scenario

A finance team asks for last quarter's revenue. A raw text-to-SQL prompt returns a number that includes cancelled orders — close enough to look right, wrong enough to misreport. Nobody notices, because the only check available is whether the SQL ran.

The hard part of natural-language analytics was never the SQL generation. It is writing down the definitions your organisation argues about somewhere a machine can read them.

Be fair to that: generation quality is genuinely good now, and that is precisely why the remaining failure mode is dangerous. The scenario is the one to sit with. A wrong revenue number that is obviously wrong gets caught. A revenue number that is three percent high because cancelled orders were included looks entirely plausible, flows into a deck, and is discovered a quarter later if at all. The only validation most home-made setups have is "did the query execute".
