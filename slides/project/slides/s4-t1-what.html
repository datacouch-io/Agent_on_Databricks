What is Databricks Genie?

Genie turns a natural-language question into SQL, runs it against tables you have explicitly allowed, and returns the rows. A Genie Agent is one curated scope: a set of tables plus the knowledge needed to query them correctly.

A space, not a chatbot

You choose the tables. Nothing outside the space is reachable.

It writes SQL

And shows it. Every answer comes with the query that produced it.

It runs on a warehouse

Your SQL warehouse, your compute, your Unity Catalog permissions.

Configured, not coded

The work is curation in a console. No prompt engineering, no deployment.

Why “curated surface” is the right phrase

A space is a deliberate narrowing: these tables, these rules, these worked examples. The narrowing is what makes the answers trustworthy, not the model behind it.

The analogy

A trained analyst who has been given a specific dataset and a briefing — rather than a very clever stranger with read access to the warehouse.

Genie is the one component in this course you configure rather than write, and that makes it easy to underestimate in both directions. Define it narrowly and accurately: a question becomes SQL, the SQL runs on your warehouse, you get rows back and you can read the query. Two things follow from that and both reassure people. First, it is auditable — there is no hidden reasoning, just a query you can review. Second, it cannot reach data you did not put in the space, and it runs under Unity Catalog permissions, so it cannot escape your existing grants either. The "curated surface" framing in the white panel is the sentence to repeat later in the session, because the next two slides are entirely about why the curation, not the model, is what makes it work. Analogy: an analyst given a dataset and a briefing, versus a clever stranger with warehouse access. Both can write SQL. Only one knows that revenue excludes cancelled orders. Likely question: "Is this text-to-SQL?" Yes, with the parts that make text-to-SQL fail in production addressed as product features — a bounded scope, stored business rules, worked examples, and a benchmark suite. That is the difference between a demo and something you would let a stakeholder use. Timing: 3 minutes.
