What is data preparation for retrieval?

The work you do in the Delta table before an index exists: splitting documents into retrievable units (chunking), choosing what gets embedded, and attaching the metadata you will filter on.

Chunk

Your rows are your chunks. There is no chunking setting — the table is the decision.

Embed

One column is embedded. If meaning spans a title and a body, concatenate upstream.

Enrich

Carry doc_id, source, version, audience, effective date. Anything you may filter on.

Govern

The table has grants, lineage and history. The index inherits from it.

An embedding is a numeric fingerprint of meaning

Similar text lands near similar text. That is why smashed finds the damaged-goods clause and a keyword index does not.

The analogy

Cutting a book into index cards. Cut by paragraph and each card stands alone. Cut by chapter and every search returns thirty pages.

This topic is in the outline and is usually treated as a preamble. It deserves its own slides, because chunking decides your retrieval ceiling and no amount of search tuning recovers from it. The first card is the framing people need: Databricks does not have a chunking setting, because your rows are your chunks. Preparation is ordinary data engineering in a Delta table, which is good news — it is work your team already knows how to do, with lineage and history for free. Define embedding properly for anyone who has not built this themselves. A numeric fingerprint of meaning, such that similar text lands near similar text. That is the entire mechanism behind the Lab 2B result where "smashed" finds the damaged-goods clause. The index-cards analogy is the one that sticks. Cut by paragraph and each card answers a question. Cut by chapter and every search hands back thirty pages, most of them irrelevant — which is a context problem as well as a retrieval one. Likely question: "Where do PDFs get parsed?" Upstream, into the table, before any of this. Extraction quality is its own problem and bad extraction is indistinguishable from bad retrieval once it is in the index. Timing: 3 minutes.
