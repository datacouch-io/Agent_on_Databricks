If you are on a trial workspace

agents.deploy() returns “Model serving is not available for trial workspaces”. Sessions 1–5 run in full; this step and one in Session 7 need Premium. Azure allows trial to premium, never the reverse.

What to check after deploying

That traces still appear, that the request log table is populating, and that the review app opens. Those three are your evidence the deployment is real.

If the workspace is Premium, deploy live — it takes four or five minutes, which is a good moment to take questions. The thing to draw attention to is how ordinary the client code is. A POST with a bearer token and a JSON body. No SDK required, no special client. That is what "it is now a service" means in practice, and it is why another team can integrate without reading your notebook. The three checks in the right-hand panel are the ones to do every time. Traces still being captured proves your instrumentation survived the packaging. The request log populating proves you have production observability. The review app opening proves you have a feedback path. Teams deploy and verify only that the endpoint returns 200, and then discover weeks later that they have no trace data. Be direct about the trial limitation. It is in the pre-flight slide for a reason — Model Serving and Agent Bricks both need Premium or Enterprise, and the upgrade is one-way on Azure, so it is a decision to make before the cohort starts rather than during this lab. Likely question: "Can I call this from outside the workspace?" Yes, with an appropriately granted principal and network configuration. That is a platform-admin conversation, not an agent one.
